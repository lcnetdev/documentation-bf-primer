# Special Terminology

The library and information space, especially the cataloging realm, is replete with special terminology. The four most loaded words are represented by the acronym WEMI: work, expression, manifestation, and item. The context in which they are used, and usually specifically defined, matters.

But the concepts these four special words represent carry general, and generally agreed upon, meaning. A “work” is understood to be some kind of artistic or intellectual creation; an “expression” is a realization of that work in some form; a “manifestation” is some kind of concrete, or physical, representation of the expression; and an “item” is a specific one of those manifestations. A synonym – and capitalization here is important – for a manifestation is “instance” (or “instantiation”). These lowercased terms are used frequently in this document and are meant, as such, to refer to these more general, basic definitions. When capitalized and/or prefixed with specific initials (indeed, used as a form of adjective), it is assumed these same words are imbued with additional, specific meaning.
